Judging the Challenge

What we would be asking of you, and what you would get for it.

This page is for people we've asked to judge. It covers what we'd be asking of you and what you'd get for it — enough to decide, and no more. The full contest rules are linked at the bottom if you want them, but you don't need them to answer us.

What the Challenge is

We built a content filter that runs on your own network, and in October we're inviting people to break it. A month-long open season: find where it fails to block what it claims to block, and tell us.

We are not doing this to collect testimonials. A product like this is only worth having if it holds up against people who genuinely try, and the only way to know whether it does is to let them try. The people who find what we missed are the most valuable people in the whole effort.

What we'd ask you to do

You never touch a router. You don't install anything, you don't reproduce anything, and you don't chase anyone for information. That work is ours.

Here's the whole of it. Someone reports a problem. We reproduce it ourselves and confirm it's real. Then it comes to you — the finding, the evidence, and our confirmation that we saw it happen — and you decide how serious it is, placing it in one of four levels from a cosmetic slip to a genuine showstopper.

That's the job. Read a confirmed finding, judge how much it matters, move on.

Most take a couple of minutes. You are judging the finding, not the person: who reported it isn't shown to you, and neither is anything else about them.

What it would cost you in time

A few hours in total, spread across the season in short sittings, whenever it suits you. Nothing arrives in the first days, it builds through October, and it tails off after the contest closes.

If more arrives than that, we filter the minor findings out ourselves rather than pass the volume on to you. Your load is our problem to manage, not yours to absorb. We would rather ask five judges for three hours each than ask two people for a fortnight.

What you'd get

A Haven-provisioned router, and a year of list updates for it, free. You should be running the thing you're helping judge.

A plaque. When the Challenge closes we'll email you about the plaque and the router, and ask where you'd like them sent — or whether you'd rather we didn't.

Your name, entirely on your terms. See below.

Your name is your call, permanently

You may serve as a judge without ever being named publicly. You may be named. You may decide the day you accept, after the results are published, next year, or never.

The choice only ever moves in the direction you choose. If you're anonymous and later want the credit, you can have it. If you're named, that doesn't reverse — so if you're unsure, start anonymous.

We'll say how many judges served either way. This is the first year of something that might turn out excellent or might turn out a mess, and nobody should have to bet their name on which before they've seen it.

Judge, or compete — not both

You're welcome to do either. A judge scoring findings they might have submitted themselves isn't a judge, so it has to be one or the other.

Your decision binds on 1 October, a month before the contest opens. Until then you can change your mind freely.

What we'll send you if you say yes

There is more structure behind this than a page like this should carry. On acceptance you'd get all of it:

  • What counts as a finding, defined precisely, so the line isn't a judgment call you have to make alone.
  • What's in scope and what isn't — a defined boundary, settled in advance rather than argued case by case.
  • Rules of engagement for participants, so you know what they agreed to.
  • How findings reach you, and what each one carries when it does.
  • The four severity levels, each with a plain-language definition, and how they feed the standings.

None of it changes what we're asking of you. It's the machinery, and it exists so that your part stays small.

The rules aren't final, and that's deliberate

The contest rules are published as proposed, not settled. We'd rather a judge shape them before agreeing to them than discover halfway through that they never signed up for what they got.

If something in them is wrong, tell us by 1 September. We read everything. We won't necessarily adopt all of it, and where we hear nothing, what's written is what we'll run.

If you'd rather just look at it

Judging isn't the only useful thing you could do. If you'd rather install it, form an opinion, and say publicly what you honestly think, that's welcome and always will be — no arrangement, no approval, nothing to sign.

Two things worth knowing if you do. The product will change under you during the contest, because we patch as findings come in. And we'd ask you not to publish a working method for defeating it while people are relying on it — say what you think of it, just don't hand out the recipe.

Saying yes, or asking first

Reply to the person who wrote to you. If you'd rather ask something before deciding, ask — including anything this page failed to answer, which we would genuinely like to know about.
